NYSE: D

Dominion Energy

Rating: BUY Sector: Regulated Electric Utilities Date: Sep 2026 Horizon: 3–5 years

Company Overview

Dominion Energy is a regulated electric utility serving Virginia, South Carolina, and North Carolina. On May 18, 2026, NextEra Energy announced it would acquire Dominion in a $67 billion, all-stock deal ($76/share, a 23% premium), with the State Corporation Commission (SCC) ruling date moved up to January 2027. Dominion is mid-way through a $65 billion capex buildout and serves electricity to the world's largest data center hub, in Northern Virginia — Virginia's contracted data center capacity has grown from 21 GW to 53.8 GW since 2024, and data centers now account for 20% of the state's electricity sales.

Investment Thesis

The fund should invest in Dominion Energy in a small-to-medium position for a 3–5 year holding period because the market is underestimating two things: the effect large-load tariffs and rate adjustment clauses (RACs) will have on Dominion's capex plans over the next five years, and the probability the NextEra merger closes. To pay for its buildout, Dominion is taking on significant debt, which the market is discounting in short-term projections — for example, when CVOW's construction was halted in December 2025, the stock fell 4%, and it recovered only 1.3% when the injunction lifted. Large-load tariffs starting in 2027 and RACs will shift much of that cost onto data center customers, converting a large share of the $65 billion capex plan into contracted, guaranteed revenue that customers must pay whether they use the power or not.

Catalysts

  • SCC ruling on the NextEra merger, now due by January 2027 (moved up from 2H 2027) — approval pushes the stock toward the $76/share deal price
  • Large-load tariffs (SCC-approved GS-5 rate class) taking effect in 2027, shifting capex risk onto 25 MW+ data center customers
  • Continued RAC approvals — e.g. Rider T1 (PJM transmission cost recovery, approved in 3 months) and Rider OSW (offshore wind cost recovery, approved in 9 months) — each converting capex into recoverable, contracted revenue
  • Sign-off from the remaining regulatory bodies: FERC, NRC, FTC, DOJ, and the VA/NC/SC state commissions

Risks & Mitigation

  • Merger requires approval from four federal bodies and three state commissions — a real risk, but early signs favor approval: South Carolina's PSC has already accepted the proposed approval schedule, and the NCUC previously approved Dominion's more complex 2018 acquisition of SCANA
  • Heavy debt load from the $65 billion capex buildout — mitigated as large-load tariffs and RACs convert an increasing share of that capex into guaranteed, contracted revenue rather than at-risk spending
  • Project-specific regulatory/construction halts (e.g. CVOW) can cause short-term stock volatility — mitigated as tariffs and RACs reduce Dominion's dependence on any single project's completion timeline

Competitive Landscape

Dominion's closest competitors are Duke Energy (FL, NC, SC, IN) and Southern Company (GA, AL, MS), with capex plans of $103 billion and $81 billion respectively. Both have introduced or proposed their own large-load tariffs and RACs, but with weaker terms and higher thresholds than Dominion's. Neither has access to the Northern Virginia data center hub, and neither is subject to a pending merger like Dominion's with NextEra.

Macro / Industry Context

Data center demand is driving a sector-wide capex buildout among regulated utilities, and large-load tariffs and RACs are emerging as the primary tools utilities in data center hubs use to shift buildout risk onto the customers driving it. Historical stock reactions suggest the market still prices in headline growth and deal news (the NextEra announcement moved the stock +9.44%) far more than the less-visible, assured revenue from tariffs and RACs (the GS-5 tariff approval moved the stock only -0.10%) — a gap that should close as more of Dominion's capex converts to contracted revenue and its valuation moves closer to lower-risk peers.

Investment Horizon & Sizing

3–5 year holding period. Small-to-medium position, sized to the binary risk around the SCC's merger ruling while still capturing the re-rating as contracted, tariff- and RAC-backed revenue grows as a share of total capex.
