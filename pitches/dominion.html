NYSE: D

Dominion Energy

Rating: BUY Sector: Regulated Electric Utilities Date: Sep 2026 Horizon: 3–5 years Position: Small-to-Medium

Company Overview

  • Regulated electric utility serving Virginia, South Carolina, and North Carolina; serves the world's largest data center hub, in Northern Virginia.
  • Undertaking a $65B capex buildout to meet surging data center demand, funded largely through new debt.
  • On May 18, 2026, NextEra Energy announced an all-stock acquisition of Dominion valued at $67B ($76/share, a 23% premium).
  • SCC ruling date on the merger moved up to January 2027 (from 2H 2027).
  • Virginia's contracted data center capacity grew from 21 GW (2024) to 53.8 GW; data centers now ~20% of Virginia's electricity sales.

Investment Thesis

The market is underestimating two things: the extent to which large-load tariffs and rate adjustment clauses (RACs) de-risk Dominion's $65B capex plan, and the probability that the NextEra merger closes. These mechanisms convert a large share of capex into contracted, guaranteed revenue — customers pay whether or not they use the power. The market currently discounts Dominion for its near-term debt load without fully pricing in this structural de-risking.

Catalysts

  • SCC ruling on the NextEra merger — due January 2027 (near-term, high-visibility event)
  • Continued contracted data center capacity growth (21 GW → 53.8 GW since 2024)
  • Additional RAC/rider approvals — Rider T1 approved in 3 months, Rider OSW in 9 months; approvals consistently land under one year
  • Merger sign-off from FERC, NRC, FTC, DOJ, and the VA/NC/SC state commissions — South Carolina's PSC has already accepted the proposed approval schedule

Risks & Mitigation

  • Risk: merger approval risk — requires sign-off from four federal bodies and three state commissions.
    Mitigation: early signals are favorable — South Carolina's PSC accepted the proposed timeline, and North Carolina previously approved Dominion's more complex 2018 SCANA acquisition.
  • Risk: high leverage from the capex buildout is pressuring near-term valuation.
    Mitigation: large-load tariffs (GS-5 rate class) and RACs shift much of this cost onto data center customers, reducing Dominion's exposure.
  • Risk: construction/regulatory setbacks (e.g. CVOW halted Dec 2025, stock fell 4%).
    Mitigation: impact was partly reversed once the injunction lifted (+1.3%); monitor SCC actions closely.

Competitive Landscape

  • Duke Energy (FL, NC, SC, IN): $103B capex plan; has introduced large-load tariffs/RACs, but with weaker terms and higher thresholds than Dominion's.
  • Southern Company (GA, AL, MS): $81B capex plan; similar tariff structure, also weaker terms than Dominion's.
  • Neither competitor has access to the Northern Virginia data center hub or is party to a transformative merger like the NextEra deal.

Macro / Industry Context

  • AI- and cloud-driven data center demand is surging, concentrated heavily in Northern Virginia — the world's largest data center hub.
  • Utilities industry-wide are adopting large-load tariffs and RACs to shift stranded-capex risk from ratepayers/investors onto large commercial customers.
  • Data centers already account for ~20% of Virginia's electricity sales, up sharply since 2024.

Merits: Why Invest Now

  • Re-rating optionality: stock trades well below the $76/share deal value, with a concentrated, near-term approval catalyst (Jan 2027 SCC ruling).
  • Structural de-risking: tariffs and RACs are shifting Dominion from merchant-style capex risk toward utility-style guaranteed cost recovery.
  • Repeated mispricing pattern: historically, the market has reacted far more to headline/deal news (+9.44% on the merger announcement) than to RAC/tariff approvals (-0.10% on GS-5 approval) — this gap is the opportunity.
  • Unique competitive moat: sole major utility with direct access to the Northern Virginia data center hub, unmatched by Duke or Southern.
  • Favorable early regulatory signals: South Carolina has already accepted the merger approval schedule, and North Carolina precedent (2018 SCANA deal) supports approval.

Investment Horizon & Sizing

Horizon: 3–5 years, spanning the SCC ruling and post-merger integration/re-rating period. Sizing: small-to-medium position — the asymmetric, near-term catalyst supports conviction, but multi-body regulatory approval risk warrants moderate, not large, sizing.
